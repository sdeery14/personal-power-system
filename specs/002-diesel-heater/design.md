## 1. Concept summary

An open, portable rectangular frame of aluminum T-slot extrusion: complete top and bottom perimeters on four continuous uprights, and a middle level of two lengthwise support rails that carry the heater crossmembers (18 members; the middle widthwise members were removed by Owner decision 2026-10-30, D-14 adopted, so there is no complete middle perimeter). The heater sits on its supplied metal plate at the middle level. The supplied 10 L tank hangs upright, broad face inward, on two lengthwise rails along one inside wall, fixed through its three factory mounting points. The lower level is open for the intake, exhaust, fuel line, and electrical connections. The frame stands on its own on its four upright ends. Supplemental stabilization hardware is excluded from the initial purchase and build and kept as a potential future enhancement (Owner decision 2026-10-29, §10); a basic stability verification (SR-10) precedes fuelled operation.

Everything in this section is an **Owner decision (2026-10-27)** except where marked. The owner and ChatGPT developed the concept; this document organizes it and records what remains open.

**Preferred preliminary layout, B1 Adjusted (Owner decision 2026-10-28).** Footprint about 20 × 22 in; height about 35 in (preliminary range 34 to 36 in). The heater stands upright on its plate with the plate underside about 7 in above the base, carried by two crossmembers that slide along the middle-level depth members. The tank stands upright beside the heater on the opposite wall, hung on two rails that slide on the uprights, with its bottom above the heater body's top by a provisional gap (D-15). This is a conservative geometric reading of the manufacturer's tank-above instruction (C12) combined with horizontal separation from the heater (C1). **It is not evidence that fuel delivery, spill protection, hot-surface separation, or the tank mounting are adequate**; SR-01 remains to be reconciled in writing (§6), every hazard in §14 stays open, and gates G1 to G5 stay closed. Two earlier layouts, A Original (tank entirely above the heater in a 38 in frame) and B1 Compact (28 in, tank overlapping the heater in height), are retained as alternatives in §16 and in the CAD model. Nothing here is a cut length. **M3 check (2026-10-30):** against the newer manual's installation figures the model as drawn places the heater body 40.8 mm from the X-max middle support rail and uprights (M3: 10 cm around the heater) and 89.2 mm from the inlet-end member plane (M3: 4 in at the air inlet); the finding, its uncertain application to an open frame, and the smallest candidate correction are in §5 (MR-08) for owner review and are not applied.

Notes:

- **Joint system (Owner decision 2026-10-30; D-01 decided as a selection, adequacy not decided).** One 2-hole inside corner bracket at each of the **28 horizontal member ends** (16 on the top and bottom perimeters, 4 on the middle support rails, 4 on the heater crossmembers, 4 on the tank rails; the continuous uprights have no bracketed ends), supplied as TNUTZ CBK-020-A kits, each stated by the supplier to contain one bracket, two M5 drop-in T-nuts, and two M5 × 8 mm button-head screws ([bom.md §2.2](bom.md#22-proposed-purchase-package-tnutz-only-prices-read-2026-10-29-and-2026-10-30)). **No doubled brackets on the loaded members, no base joining plates, no reinforcement plates**, and no foot bars or outriggers in the initial design. One supplier for all structural extrusion and joining hardware (TNUTZ), so the cross-supplier fit question of 2026-10-29 no longer arises; what TNUTZ does and does not state about its own parts is in bom.md §2.1. *Historical (2026-10-29, superseded):* ZYLtech 2028 brackets doubled on the four loaded members, eight 60 × 60 × 4 mm joining plates at the base corners, and M5 hammer nuts, first as an all-ZYLtech family and then as a hybrid with TNUTZ extrusion. Reasons that carry over: no drilling or tapping of extrusion ends, cut-length error is absorbed in the joint, and the crossmembers and rails can slide to the real plate pitch and boss rise. End-tapped blind fasteners are no longer under consideration. **Working engineering assumption (Owner decision 2026-10-30):** the specified bracket arrangement is treated as sufficiently rigid for preliminary geometry and procurement planning. This is an assumption, not verified joint strength or stability, and not authorization to operate the heater; no further structural comparison of joint options is to be made unless an MR-05 or SR-10 finding requires one. **Load paths under this approach:** heater through Interface A and B (§5) into the crossmembers, through their single brackets into the middle support rails, through those rails' brackets into the uprights and through their lower ends to the ground; tank through its three mounting points into the rail T-nuts, through the rail brackets into the uprights; lateral loads through the bottom and top rectangles and the bracket joints alone, since there are no base plates and no middle widthwise members. **Material concern recorded for the MR-05 review (not a reopening of the joint selection):** with single 2-hole brackets, no base plates, and no closed middle rectangle, the frame's racking stiffness and its resistance to a handling push rest entirely on the rotational stiffness of 28 small bracket joints of unstated material, thickness, and rating, the slip resistance of the drop-in T-nuts carrying the crossmembers and rails, and the top and bottom rectangles; the 2020 section's upright bending stiffness adds to this. These are the suspected governing concerns, with the plastic tank mounting points (§6). With the illustrative masses in §10 the bending moments in the crossmembers and rails are expected to be small, but member bending strength and deflection are **not excluded**: the only calculation that exists is the preliminary sensitivity set in the next bullet, made on assumed loads and supports with no acceptance criteria, and the review must cover every loaded member on a stated basis. The profile choice is not reopened by the review: if the frame is found wanting at MR-05 or at the SR-10 verification, the remedy is bracing or added members, recorded under D-01 and MR-01, not a change of section. MR-05 and A-04 stay open, and the supplier evidence listed in [bom.md §2.1](bom.md#21-supplier-compatibility-criteria-evidence-to-obtain-before-g1) is required before any adequacy statement.
- **MR-05 preliminary sensitivity calculations (Proposed, 2026-10-29; hypothetical cases at concept level, not a structural acceptance and not a demonstration that any member passes).** Made with the TNUTZ EXM-2020 published section properties (Ix = Iy = 0.67 cm⁴, area 1.64 cm², 0.454 kg/m; Manufacturer-stated for that profile only, bom.md §2.1). **No acceptance criteria exist:** no allowable stress, allowable deflection, load factor, or governing load case has been set by review, so the figures below are compared with nothing, and no member is reported as passing or failing. **Assumptions, every one of them open:** E = 69 GPa (a generic aluminum value, not a supplier figure); extreme-fibre distance 10 mm, so Z = 670 mm³; member ends treated as pinned (conservative for member moment, silent about the joints); a hypothetical heater-plus-plate load of 5 kg, an **independent illustrative assumption chosen for sensitivity only**, numerically equal to the C22 net weight but not derived from it, since A-06 records that the C22 figure's scope is unclarified and it is not to be used as the heater mass in load or stability calculations; that 5 kg shared equally by the two crossmembers as a mid-span point load; a hypothetical full tank of 10 kg (A-05 fuel plus an assumed 1.5 kg empty tank, MAS-03 not recorded) carried entirely by the lower rail at the two lower points 173 mm apart (User-reported spacing); a handling multiplier of 2 and a road-transport multiplier of 3 g as outline cases, neither set by review. The profile's yield strength is **not stated by the supplier**, and no allowable stress or deflection is inferred from supplier data; a typical 6063-T5 yield of about 145 MPa is mentioned only as a scale for reading the stresses, not as a limit. **Arithmetic (independently verified at the PR #11 review):** heater crossmember, 468 mm span, 25 N at mid-span: about 4 MPa and 0.1 mm. Lower tank rail, 518.8 mm span, 49 N at each lower point: about 13 MPa and 0.5 mm; with the handling multiplier about 25 MPa and 1.1 mm; with 3 g about 38 MPa and 1.6 mm. Uprights: axial stress below 1 MPa; pinned-column Euler load about 5.8 kN against about 53 N applied. **Hypothetical lateral case, idealized:** a 100 N horizontal push at the top (889 mm), with the four uprights treated as cantilevers fixed at a rigid base and sharing the load equally (25 N each), gives about 33 MPa in each upright, about 13 mm of sway, and about 22 N·m at each base corner. The fixed-base and equal-sharing assumptions are idealizations, not an upper bound on the real frame: since 2026-10-30 each base corner is held only by the two single 2-hole brackets of the two bottom perimeter members that meet there (no joining plate, material, thickness, and rating unstated), the top rectangle changes the upright curvature, and the load need not share equally; with no middle widthwise members the uprights also have no intermediate widthwise restraint. The 100 N is an arbitrary hypothetical handling load and is **distinct from the about 41 N illustrative tipping threshold in §10** (about 47 N before the 2026-10-30 recomputation): the two come from different assumptions, neither is a criterion, and under the §10 assumptions the module would tip toward the tank wall before a 100 N top push was reached. The member figures are unchanged by the 2026-10-30 decisions: removing `L2-W1` and `L2-W2` changes no loaded member's span or load, and the bracket change affects the joints, not the member arithmetic. **What the calculations show:** how the assumed loads scale into member stresses and deflections for this section; for these hypothetical cases the member figures are small relative to typical aluminum strengths. **What they do not show:** that any member, joint, or the frame is adequate. Bracket-joint stiffness and strength, the drop-in T-nuts' slip load (not stated by the supplier), racking stiffness, and the acceptance criteria themselves are all unresolved; no whole-frame adequacy is stated or implied. MR-05 and A-04 remain open until criteria are set at review and a joint figure, a sample-joint result, or an explicit G2 acceptance on a stated basis exists; the owner's working rigidity assumption (A-04) does not close them.
- **Section (D-02 closed 2026-10-29).** Every member is the same 2020 square section, so no member has a section orientation to choose; each face carries one 6 mm slot. The preferred orientations recorded for a 20 × 40 mm section are withdrawn with it. The CAD draws every member 20 × 20 mm.
- **18-member frame (D-14 adopted, Owner decision 2026-10-30).** The two middle-level widthwise members (`L2-W1`, `L2-W2`) are removed; the middle level is the two lengthwise support rails `L2-D1` and `L2-D2` with the heater crossmembers `HX1` and `HX2` butting between them, so there is no complete middle perimeter. The owner reports (2026-10-28, not measured) four holes about 1/4 in diameter with about 1/8 in from each hole's outside edge to its two adjacent plate edges, so the hole centres are about 1/4 in in from each edge and the centre-to-centre pitch is about 4 15/16 in along the 5 7/16 in side and about 6 3/16 in along the 6 11/16 in side. ## 5. Heater mounting

- Heater product size 13.4 × 4.5 × 5.5 in / 340 × 115 × 140 mm (Manufacturer-stated, C21; the 13.4 in length also User-reported by tape measure). Net weight 11 lb / 5 kg of unstated scope (C22, Q-11); the standalone heater mass remains Unknown.
- The supplied metal plate (5 7/16 × 6 11/16 in, User-reported) mounts on H-X. M1 p.9 shows a seal between the heater and the plate and "double head screw nut" fixings; M1 p.8 lists four machine fixing screws and one machine fixing piece. The owner reports (2026-10-28, not measured) four holes about 1/4 in diameter about 1/8 in from each adjacent edge, and a thickness of about 1/32 in, below 1/16 in. - **Two mechanical interfaces, one unresolved load path (D-16).** *Interface A, heater to its supplied plate*: the factory fixing arrangement, M1's seal and "double head screw nut" fixings with four machine fixing screws (M1 p.8, p.9). Its geometry, where it bears on the plate, and how it transfers the heater's load into the plate are incompletely established (Q-03; HTR-11, HTR-16, PLT-12). *Interface B, plate to frame*: provisionally, the plate's four corner holes (User-reported 2026-10-28) bolted with M5 button-head screws and flat washers into M5 drop-in T-nuts in the two crossmembers' top slots (TNUTZ DB-020-M5, four of the ten additional nuts, Owner decision 2026-10-30; the screws are not in the purchase package. **Provisional starting length (owner, 2026-10-30): M5 × 8 mm button-head screws with flat washers, Proposed, not verified.** With the plate at about 0.8 mm (an estimate, PLT-03) and a washer of roughly 1 mm, about 6 mm of screw would remain for the slot lip and the T-nut; whether that engages the nut's full thread, or a longer screw would bottom on the slot floor before clamping, depends on the DB-020-M5 nut height and thread length and the EXM-2020 lip thickness and cavity depth, none of which TNUTZ states (bom.md §2.1). The length is therefore to be specified before G1 from the plate as found at the dry fit and a pre-purchase basis for the nut and slot dimensions (a TNUTZ drawing or inquiry answer, or a documented engineering assessment; bom.md §2.1); checking the delivered nut and screw in the delivered extrusion is a G2 acceptance check of that basis, not its source, and if the basis cannot be obtained G1(e) stays open for this item, [bom.md §2.2](bom.md#22-proposed-purchase-package-tnutz-only-prices-read-2026-10-29-and-2026-10-30)). Whether the plate, Interface A, Interface B, and the crossmembers together carry the heater under static, handling, and transport loads is **unresolved** until the interfaces, the connection details, and the loads have been assessed (MR-03, MR-05).

- Power must not be cut during operation or the 3–5 minute cool-down (C4, C8). This rules out, until reviewed, any upstream device that could open the circuit during cool-down: a low-voltage disconnect, a battery protection trip, a switched branch, or a connector that can be unplugged under load (SR-08).

The V1 fuse block has no assigned branch ([as-built.md §4.4](../001-battery-box/as-built.md#44-branch-assignments)) and the box carries open safety concerns. The interface is a Battery Box V2 requirement (ER-03). The controller's engineering mode shows supply voltage (M1 p.14), which will help characterize cable drop when that work is done.
- The heater must not be started from a cigarette-lighter socket or plug (C20, ER-05). No supply cable, fuse, or connector has been selected; that selection belongs to the Battery Box V2 interface and gate G3.
- Where the controller and its cable are mounted on the frame, and how the harness is protected from the exhaust, are open (D-09).
